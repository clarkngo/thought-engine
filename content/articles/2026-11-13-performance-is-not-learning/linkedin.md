# LinkedIn copy: Performance Is Not Learning

## Post

Over the past three weeks I've posted the same pattern from different angles.

Students with ChatGPT-style help scored 48% higher in practice. Then 17% lower on the exam once the AI was gone.

A structured AI tutor at Harvard more than doubled median learning gains over an active-learning class.

Same kind of technology. Opposite results.

Half of 885 intro programming students switched off their AI tutor's guardrails at least once. Lower performers were more likely to, often close to the deadline.

One CS1 course let students use AI and added weekly oral code reviews. AI use went up. Exam averages held steady.

The thread through all of it is old learning science:

Performance is what students can do with help.

Learning is what remains without it.

AI widens the gap between the two. And most gradebooks only measure the first.

I've written a longer, citable piece on it, with four design principles:

• Make the student do the step.
• Measure without the tool at least once.
• Assess the explanation, not only the artifact.
• Build judgment, not trust.

It also covers what this means for teaching centers, program review, AI procurement, and institutional research.

If your grade distributions rose this year, how would you know whether learning did?
