# LinkedIn copy: Teach Verification, Not Syntax

## Post

An intro CS course let students use AI.

AI use went up.

Exam scores didn't go down.

Here's what they changed:

Every week, students sat for a short oral code review. They explained the code they submitted. Whoever, or whatever, wrote it.

Keystroke logs showed significantly more pasted code than in past semesters. Yet exam averages showed no statistically significant change.

The insight isn't "allow AI" or "ban AI."

The assessment moved from the artifact to the explanation.

If a student can defend the code, you have evidence of learning. Whoever typed it.

The field is moving the same way. A 2026 report from 23 computing educators and practitioners argues that coding, debugging, and documentation are losing their central place. What gains: verifying AI-generated work, system design, abstraction, and critical evaluation.

Assess the explanation, not the artifact:

• Short oral code reviews, every week. Five minutes with a TA scales better than another detector.
• Teach verification as a skill. Reading code, writing tests, spotting the plausible-but-wrong answer.
• Re-center the fundamentals. You can't verify what you don't understand.

(Caveat: one course, a preprint. A design worth piloting, not a proven formula.)
