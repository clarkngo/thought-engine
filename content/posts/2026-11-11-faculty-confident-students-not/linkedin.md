# LinkedIn copy: Faculty Are Most Confident Where Students Are Least

## Post

Only 19% of US and Canadian students say their program feels current for an AI world.

58% of their faculty aren't worried their teaching will go out of date.

That's from the Digital Education Council's 2026 global survey. Globally the gap is smaller, but it points the same way.

Students' doubts are specific. Only 28% say most of their assessments reflect the work they expect to do in an AI-enabled workplace.

And policy lags practice. Lumina–Gallup finds 57% of U.S. college students use AI in coursework every week, while 53% say their school discourages or prohibits it.

Here's the mechanism.

Faculty judge "current" by content: is what I teach still true?

Students judge it by the job market: will this help me do the work?

Both are reasonable. Only one of them is applying for jobs.

Let the market grade the curriculum:

• Ask students and employers every term whether the course feels current.
• Rewrite one assessment per course to mirror how the work is done with AI.
• Publish what changed, so students can see the program moving.

Who should decide whether a curriculum is current: faculty, students, or employers?
