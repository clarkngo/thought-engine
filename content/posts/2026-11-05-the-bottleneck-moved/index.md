Drafting a lesson now takes minutes. So why did my course build still take nine weeks?

I've been building a new course with an AI assistant as my build partner. Lessons, starter code, rubrics, quiz banks: each one drafted in minutes.

The slow part moved. It became keeping everything consistent. The syllabus, the student site, the LMS, and the rubrics all had to say the same thing.

They often didn't. A fix made in one copy never reached the master, and the next edit brought the old error back: Week 8 instead of Week 7, 40 points instead of 50.

The other slow part was decisions. The course changed shape twice. With AI, each reshape took days, not a term. But every reshape started with a decision only I could make.

What I'd tell anyone building a course with AI:

- **Lock the non-negotiables before any content exists.**
- **Give every artifact one source of truth.** Link to it; don't copy it.
- **Use AI as a checker, not just a writer.** Its most valuable work was finding mismatches between documents.

Building is no longer the bottleneck. Deciding and staying consistent are. It's the same lesson as [The Seams Are the System](/articles/2026-10-21-the-seams-are-the-system/): the failures live between the parts.
