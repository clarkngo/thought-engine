# LinkedIn copy: Start Small. The Ideas Come Later.

## Post

The question I get most about AI isn't "will it take my job?"

It's "where do I start?"

Students ask. Colleagues ask. People outside work ask.

My answer is always the same: build something small.

Something personal. Something for work. Something for a hobby. Something almost embarrassingly simple.

Not because the first project matters.

Because the tenth one does.

Then I show them my own projects. Not as a standard to meet, but as proof that most of it started small.

Somewhere along the way, two things happen. You start seeing ideas everywhere. And you get better at working with AI: what to ask, what to check, when it's wrong.

I ran a vibe coding workshop built on the same idea.

On Monday I wrote that AI complements the knowledge you only get from practice. This is the personal version of that. Nobody gets proficient from a slide deck.

• Start smaller than feels useful.
• Build something you'll actually use.
• Keep going past the first one. The ideas come from building, not before it.

What was the first thing you built with AI, and what did it teach you?
