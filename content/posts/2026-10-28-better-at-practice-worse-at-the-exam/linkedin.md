# LinkedIn copy: Better at Practice. Worse at the Exam.

## Post

Students with ChatGPT-style help scored 48% higher in practice.

Then 17% lower on the exam.

That's from a randomized field experiment published in PNAS. Nearly 1,000 students got one of three things: no AI, a standard ChatGPT-style assistant, or a "tutor" version with guardrails. Hints instead of answers. Teacher input.

During practice, AI looked like a miracle.

ChatGPT-style: +48%.
Guardrailed tutor: +127%.

Then the AI was taken away for the exam.

The ChatGPT-style group scored 17% below students who never had AI.

The tutor group's drop essentially disappeared. But it didn't beat the no-AI group either.

Practice is supposed to build the skill. When the tool does the thinking, the score goes up and the skill doesn't.

Performance is not learning.

And this isn't just a high school problem. In the Digital Education Council's 2026 global survey, 19% of university students say they're retaining less, and 22% find it harder to work without AI.

One honest caveat: one trial, one subject, one country. And even the best design only prevented harm. Guardrails are the floor, not the finish line.

Design for learning, not performance:

• Tutors give hints, not answers.
• Assess without AI at least once, so you can see what stuck.
• Grade the reasoning, not just the output.

If your students' homework scores went up this year, how do you know they learned more?
