# LinkedIn copy: Practice Without Feedback Made Them Worse

## Post

Trainee counselors practiced with an AI patient.

The ones who practiced alone came out less empathetic than they went in.

That's from a randomized study by a Stanford team, published at CHI 2026.

94 novice counselors. Two groups.

One practiced with an AI-simulated patient.

The other did the same practice, plus AI feedback on what they did.

Practice alone: no gain in the skills that matter. Empathy went down.

Practice with feedback: reflections and questions improved. On empathy, they ended well ahead of the other group.

Same simulator. Same patient. Same twenty minutes.

The only difference was whether anyone told them what they did.

This matters because AI job simulators are the obvious answer to the missing first job. If juniors stop getting reps at work, simulate the work.

AI patients for medical students. AI witnesses for law associates. AI clients and customers for everyone else.

But a simulator without feedback isn't neutral.

Novices fall back on instinct. The AI patient never says "you missed what I was feeling." So the instinct gets rehearsed.

Reps without correction can train the wrong habit.

Before you buy a job simulator:

• No simulator without a debrief. Practice alone can rehearse the wrong habit.
• Feedback on the move, not the score. "Name the feeling first" teaches. "72/100" doesn't.
• Measure the skill, not the sessions. Hours in the simulator are not a learning outcome.

(Caveat: one 75-minute session with novices. Short-term effects.)

If AI can play the patient, the client, and the customer, who should play the coach: the AI, the instructor, or both?

## First comment

🔗 Louie, Shah, Hasan Orney, Pacheco, Brunskill & Yang (Stanford / Georgia Tech), "Can LLM-Simulated Practice and Feedback Upskill Human Counselors? A Randomized Study with 90+ Novice Counselors," CHI 2026: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02428

Notes on the numbers: 94 novice counselors, randomized to practice with an AI patient alone or with AI feedback; a 20-minute practice chat inside a 75-minute online session. Feedback group: reflections and questions improved (d = 0.32–0.39). Practice-only group: empathy declined (d = −0.52); between groups, the feedback group was ahead on empathy (d = 0.72). "The skills that matter" means the client-centered microskills the authors measured; the practice-only group did change on one other measure (suggestions). The feedback was AI-generated, so this doesn't compare AI and human coaching.
