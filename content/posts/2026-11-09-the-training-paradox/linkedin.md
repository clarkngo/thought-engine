# LinkedIn copy: The Training Paradox

## Post

64% of faculty have taken AI literacy training.

Only 29% of students think their instructors can guide them on AI.

In the US and Canada: 17%.

That's from the Digital Education Council's 2026 global survey. 45,398 responses across 35 countries.

North America stands out. Faculty intent to use AI in teaching fell from 76% to 67% in a year, the lowest of any region. And 43% of US and Canadian students say they'd support an institution-wide AI ban.

The report's diagnosis: most students can't say whether their assessments even permit AI.

So the training is happening.

It just isn't reaching the place students look: their assignments.

Tool tours build faculty familiarity. Students need clear rules and visible practice in the work they're graded on.

Readiness is what students can see:

• Put an AI policy on every assignment, not just the syllabus. Allowed, limited, or not at all, and why.
• Train for course redesign, not tool tours. Measure training by what changed in a course.
• Show students how you use AI yourself. Modeling is guidance.

Faculty: what did your AI training actually change in your course?
