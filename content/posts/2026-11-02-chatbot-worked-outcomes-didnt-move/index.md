A chatbot text more than doubled on-time registration. Four years later, graduation hadn't moved.

That's from a [randomized trial at Cal State Northridge](https://doi.org/10.26300/b0dp-sn77) that followed 8,708 students for four years. Among students who hadn't registered, one reminder lifted on-time enrollment from 32% to 66%. Students stayed receptive to the chatbot throughout.

The long-run results: no detectable effect on GPA, credits, persistence, or graduation. Four-year graduation was about 19% in both groups. The authors call these nulls "precisely estimated." The study was large enough to see a small effect.

On the [Engaged by Design podcast](https://www.engagedbydesign.com/media/podcast), Dr. Eric Klein of American College of Education described the hopeful version: AI takes on routine tasks, which frees time for mentoring and human connection. I agree with the goal. This trial shows the second half doesn't happen on its own.

Compare [CUNY ASAP](https://mdrc.org/node/16044), a human-heavy bundle that includes high-touch advising: 40.1% graduated in three years, against 21.8% of the control group.

Freed time is not reinvested time:

- **Automate the deadline.** Reminders and repeat questions are where a chatbot earns its keep.
- **Decide where the hours go before you buy.** Name the advising or mentoring they will fund.
- **Measure outcomes, not messages.** Track persistence, not texts sent.

One honest caveat: this is one university, with a pre-programmed chatbot rather than generative AI, and ASAP's effect can't be credited to advising alone.
