# LinkedIn copy: The Chatbot Worked. The Outcomes Didn't Move.

## Post

A chatbot text more than doubled on-time registration.

Four years later, graduation hadn't moved.

That's from a randomized trial at Cal State Northridge that followed 8,708 students for four years.

Among students who hadn't registered, one reminder lifted on-time enrollment from 32% to 66%.

Students stayed receptive to the chatbot throughout.

The long-run results?

No detectable effect on GPA, credits, persistence, or graduation.

Four-year graduation: about 19% in both groups.

The study was large enough to see a small effect. The authors call these nulls "precisely estimated."

On the Engaged by Design podcast, Dr. Eric Klein of American College of Education described the hopeful version: AI takes on routine tasks, which frees time for mentoring and human connection.

I agree with the goal.

This trial shows the second half doesn't happen on its own.

Compare CUNY ASAP, a human-heavy bundle that includes high-touch advising. 40.1% graduated in three years, against 21.8% of the control group.

Freed time is not reinvested time:

• Automate the deadline. Reminders and repeat questions are where a chatbot earns its keep.
• Decide where the hours go before you buy. Name the advising or mentoring they will fund.
• Measure outcomes, not messages. Track persistence, not texts sent.

(Fair caveat: one university, and a pre-programmed chatbot rather than generative AI. ASAP's effect can't be credited to advising alone.)

Where did the last hours your team saved actually go?

## First comment

🔗 Mata, Russell & Page (2026), "Sustaining AI-Enabled Student Support: A Four-Year Implementation and Impact Study," EdWorkingPaper 26-1409: https://doi.org/10.26300/b0dp-sn77
🔗 MDRC on CUNY ASAP: https://mdrc.org/node/16044
🎧 Engaged by Design podcast: https://www.engagedbydesign.com/media/podcast

The 32% to 66% is derived from the paper's Table 3: control mean 31.5% plus a 34.2-point effect, among the 621 students who hadn't yet registered by the July 2018 campaign. Most students in both groups registered by mid-September, so the reminder mainly moved registration earlier.

Four-year graduation: 19.0% control; the 0.4-point difference isn't statistically significant.
