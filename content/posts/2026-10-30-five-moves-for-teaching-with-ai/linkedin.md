# LinkedIn copy: Five Moves for Teaching With AI

## Post

This week: AI in a course can lower exam scores. Or double learning gains.

The difference wasn't the model. It was the design.

Five moves any instructor can make next term:

1. Hints, not answers.
A ChatGPT-style assistant cut exam scores 17%. The guardrailed tutor in the same trial didn't.

2. Sequence the work.
Harvard's instructor-built tutor walked students one step at a time. Median learning gains more than doubled over an active-learning class.

3. Assess without AI at least once.
It's how you see what stuck.

4. Grade the explanation, not the artifact.
One intro CS course added weekly oral code reviews. AI use rose. Exam averages held steady.

5. Teach verification as a skill.
A 2026 report from 23 computing educators says verifying AI-generated work is gaining ground as writing code by hand loses it.

Only 5% of students with AI in their courses say it transformed how they learn (Digital Education Council, 2026).

Adding AI isn't redesigning.

Which of the five would be hardest to pull off at your institution?
