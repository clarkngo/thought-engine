# LinkedIn copy: Same AI. Opposite Results.

## Post

Two randomized trials.

The same kind of AI.

Opposite results.

Trial one (PNAS): nearly 1,000 high school math students. Those who practiced with a ChatGPT-style assistant scored 17% lower on the exam than students who never had AI.

Trial two (Harvard, Scientific Reports): 194 undergraduates in intro physics. A structured AI tutor at home produced a median learning gain more than double that of an in-class active-learning lesson. In less time.

So what changed?

The design.

The Harvard tutor was built by the instructors. It walked students through each part of each problem, one step at a time. Its prompts encoded how people learn. Its solutions were written by experts so it stayed accurate.

The other assistant just answered whatever it was asked.

"Does AI help students learn?" is the wrong question.

The right one: what does the AI make the student do?

Design decides the direction:

• Sequence the work. One step at a time, with the student doing each step.
• Write the expert prompts and answers yourself. Don't outsource your pedagogy to a default chatbot.
• Measure learning without the AI, not performance with it.

(Fair caveat: different ages, subjects, and measures. These trials point the same way about design, not about effect sizes.)

If you could rebuild one assignment around a structured AI tutor, which one would it be?
