# LinkedIn copy: Create Comes First

## Post

Bloom's taxonomy puts Create at the top.

In the course I'm designing, it comes first.

Here's why.

In Anthropic's analysis of 574,740 student conversations, 39.8% of Claude's responses were at the "Creating" level. Just 1.8% were "Remembering."

The top of the pyramid is now the cheap part.

So students use an AI assistant to get a working version fast.

That's 20% of the grade.

The other 80% goes where the research says the learning is:

• Evaluate: test whether the AI is actually right. In a Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon survey, critical thinking with AI shifted toward verification. And more confidence in AI went with less of it.

• Analyze: explain how it works, in your own words. One intro CS course added weekly oral code reviews. AI use rose. Exam averages held steady.

• Refine: extend it by hand, no AI. In a randomized trial in PNAS, students with ChatGPT-style help scored 48% higher in practice, then 17% lower once the AI was removed.

If AI can write working code, "does it run?" grades the AI, not the student.

The course hasn't met students yet, so now is the time to poke holes in it.

Where would Create-first break down in your field?
