# LinkedIn copy: 67% Less Escalation

## Post

40 people trained for a conflict.

One group studied a lecture on conflict strategy.

The other rehearsed with an AI that played the other side.

Then each faced a real conflict. No AI.

The rehearsal group used 67% fewer escalating tactics. And twice as many cooperative ones.

That's Stanford's Rehearsal study. The AI didn't just argue back. It showed "what if" paths and gave feedback on which strategy to use, and when.

Same lesson as yesterday: reps plus feedback.

What hard conversation would you want students to rehearse before they have it for real?

## First comment

🔗 Shaikh, Chai, Gelfand, Yang & Bernstein (Stanford), "Rehearsal: Simulating Conflict to Teach Conflict Resolution," CHI 2024: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12309

Notes on the numbers: 40 participants, between-subjects, compared with a lecture group that covered the same conflict theory (Interest-Rights-Power). After training, each had a real conflict with a trained confederate. The 67% and "doubling" are the paper's reported average changes. A small lab study; short-term effects.
