# AI honesty penalty: research notes (Nov 22, 2026)

**Reif, Larrick & Soll, PNAS 2025** (doi 10.1073/pnas.2426766122; read on PMC12088386)
- Four preregistered online experiments, N = 4,439. AI users "anticipate and receive negative evaluations regarding their competence and motivation."
- Study 1 (N = 497): imagined AI-tool users expected to look lazier (3.25 vs 2.72) and were less willing to disclose to managers and colleagues (self-reported willingness, hypothetical).
- Study 2 (N = 1,203): AI help rated lazier than non-AI help (2.50 vs 2.16) and control (2.02); also less competent, diligent, independent. No demographic moderation.
- Study 3 (managers, ~1,667): average task fit similar; hiring depended on the manager's own AI use. Low-AI managers favored the non-AI candidate; heavy-AI managers favored the daily-AI candidate.
- Study 4 (N = 1,002): AI penalty on task fit for manual tasks; offset for a digital task where AI was useful. Laziness mediated. Among evaluators using AI weekly/daily, the laziness difference was not significant.
- Data collected Mar 2024–Feb 2025; authors expect the penalty to shift.

**Atlassian Teamwork Lab, June 2026** (vendor research, blog post only)
- 961 U.S. full-time knowledge workers, Mar 30–Apr 7, 2026. Same email, varied AI-disclosure note.
- Disclosure: "10x lazier" (odd metric, not used) and 24 percentage points less likely to recommend for a high-visibility project.
- "For the team" framing helped (recommend 43% → 51%) but stayed below no-mention. Where AI is "celebrated," stigma nearly disappears.
- Pulse survey (1,006): 94% use AI; nearly 80% transparent.

**Charter, 2026** (seen only via Inc./The Star, Oct 28, 2026; find the primary before using)
- 5,000 U.S. workers: 35.9% concealed AI use from a manager, colleague, or client. Managers 43.7%, executives 37.1%, ICs 27.1%.
- Reasons: 49.3% fear looking less capable; 35.5% lazy; 26% unsure it's allowed.

**METR RCT, July 2025** (for Wed): 16 experienced developers, 246 tasks; 19% slower with AI; expected 24% faster, believed 20% faster afterward. Feb 2026 update: some evidence of speedup, but selection effects make the estimate unreliable.
