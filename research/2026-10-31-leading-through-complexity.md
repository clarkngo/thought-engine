# Research notes: Leading higher ed through complexity (2026-10-31)

Prompted by Clark listening to the Engaged by Design podcast with Dr. Eric Klein (Assistant Provost, American College of Education), "Willingness Before Certainty." Clark shared a summary, not the audio. The episode wasn't found by web search, so get its link from Clark and don't quote Klein directly until checked against the episode.

Themes tested: complexity accumulates through reasonable decisions; willingness before certainty; AI automates routine work to free time for people; simplify to personalize.

Verified against primary sources unless marked.

## Mata, Russell & Page (July 2026), EdWorkingPaper 26-1409, "Sustaining AI-Enabled Student Support: A Four-Year Implementation and Impact Study" (full PDF read)
- https://doi.org/10.26300/b0dp-sn77 · California State University, Northridge. RCT over four years, Fall 2018 and Fall 2019 cohorts; N = 8,708 (about 4,361 treatment, 4,347 control). 69% Pell eligible, 51% first generation, 66% Hispanic.
- Registration reminder: treated students 34 percentage points more likely to enroll by Aug 16 (second reminder: 2 points). Early Start: 11 points after the May 25 text, 20 points after the June 8 reminder. Campaign-level results, not the overall effect.
- No statistically significant difference in enrollment, persistence, graduation rates, credits, or term/cumulative GPA, including for female, Hispanic, Pell-eligible and first-generation subgroups. Authors: well powered (MDES 0.05 SD), so nulls are "precisely estimated."
- Authors: tools "still require significant human engagement"; robust institution-level data systems are a precursor; such communication "may be insufficient on its own." Pair with complementary strategies.
- Sustainability needs centralized ownership. Student data isn't centralized: a FAFSA campaign needed lists passed between offices. (Echoes "The Seams Are the System.")
- The AI answers instantly from a pre-approved knowledge base, freeing staff from repeat questions.
- Caveats: **non-generative** chatbot (pre-programmed knowledge base), one university, spillover possible (COVID-era expansion), control never had the chatbot.

## AIR with EDUCAUSE, NACUBO, CUPA-HR (Jan 13, 2026), "AI at Work in Higher Education" (AIR summary read; full report not)
- http://www.airweb.org/article/2026/01/14/ai-at-work-in-higher-education · 1,960 respondents, Sept 29–Oct 13, 2025.
- 94% used AI tools for work in the past six months; 54% aware of policies or guidelines governing that use.
- 92% say their institution has a work-related AI strategy; 13% say it measures ROI for work-related AI tools.
- 56% have used AI tools not provided by their institution for work. 11% are required to use AI.
- Top opportunities: automating repetitive processes, offloading administrative and routine tasks, analyzing large datasets. Top challenges: pace of change, lack of expertise, lack of best practices.
- Caveat: individual perceptions, not institutional records; respondents may not know whether ROI is measured.

## Dynarski, Libassi, Michelmore & Owen (2018), NBER WP 25349, "Closing the Gap" (HAIL scholarship, abstract and NBER digest read)
- https://www.nber.org/papers/w25349 · Randomized; about 4,000 high-achieving, low-income Michigan students over a two-year pilot.
- "The intervention did not increase aid": students were guaranteed before applying the grant aid they would qualify for in expectation after admission. Four years of free tuition and no obligation to file aid forms.
- Applied: two-thirds vs a quarter. Enrolled: 27% vs 12% (NBER digest). A U-M release says 26% vs 12%; use the digest and check the paper.
- Authors attribute this to uncertainty, present bias, and loss aversion.
- Caveats: one flagship university, high-achieving students, a glossy mailed package; published AER version not checked.

## MDRC (2015), CUNY ASAP evaluation (MDRC release read; full report not)
- https://mdrc.org/node/16044 · Randomized; low-income students needing one or two developmental courses at three CUNY community colleges.
- Three-year associate's degree rate: 40.1% ASAP vs 21.8% control (18.3 points).
- A bundle: high-touch advising, block-scheduled classes, seminars, career services, transit cards, free textbooks. Effects can't be credited to advising alone. MDRC reports advisers met students twice a month during the evaluation.

## Weaker / secondary (verify before use)
